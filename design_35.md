# Design 35 - Telikom Pulse

## Status
New-round concept specification for `35.html`. This is not a continuation or refinement of designs 1 to 34.

## Portfolio position
- Gen Z affinity: **9/10**
- Enterprise / premium: **8/10**
- UX clarity: **10/10**
- PNG identity: **7/10**
- Above-the-fold utility: **10/10**
- Motion: **7/10**
- Code complexity: **7/10**
- Similarity target vs designs 1 to 34: **0/10 in first-viewport composition and interaction**

## Core premise
Turn the Telikom homepage into a polished action hub that happens to contain a powerful campaign hero.

The first screen should solve the reviewer's biggest concern: there must be an immediate reason to stay on the page before scrolling.

Instead of asking the visitor to read a large marketing banner and then discover useful links later, this design gives them both at once:

- a strong visual campaign;
- a concise value proposition;
- immediate account / service actions;
- main product entry points;
- one credibility signal.

This concept takes inspiration from the utility-first behaviour of Ooredoo and the single-message promotional clarity of STC / AT&T, but it must look unmistakably Telikom.

## First viewport concept
Desktop should feel like one integrated composition, not a hero with unrelated cards glued underneath.

### Layer 1: slim utility header
Very restrained top strip:
- Personal
- Business
- Support
- Find a Store / Contact
- Search
- Self Care

Keep this visually lighter than the primary navigation.

### Layer 2: primary header
- Large Telikom logo.
- Slogan clearly readable.
- Mobile.
- Internet.
- Business.
- Offers.
- About.
- One filled Self Care / Login button if appropriate.

Header can be white or very pale neutral. Avoid an all-blue nav bar.

### Layer 3: campaign hero
Use approximately 65 to 72 percent of desktop width for the campaign story and 28 to 35 percent for the action console.

Hero copy example structure only, not final approved copy:

**H1:** Connected for what comes next.

One short support sentence of no more than roughly 16 words.

Primary CTA: Explore Plans
Secondary text link: Why Telikom

The image should be an exceptional PNG-relevant photograph or campaign composition showing a young adult / family / creator / business user in a recognisable local context. It should be bright and properly art-directed, not a generic stock portrait.

Use Telikom blue as graphic framing, typography or edge detail. Do not put a heavy blue filter over the entire image.

### Layer 4: Pulse Actions console
A distinct right-side utility panel occupying the full hero height or most of it.

Heading:
**What do you need today?**

Four large action rows with icons:
1. Top Up / Recharge
2. Self Care
3. Mobile & Data
4. Internet / Broadband

Then one smaller support row:
**Need help? Get Support**

Each action must look immediately clickable and must have a large touch target.

Do not use tiny pill buttons. Think of a modern airport / fintech service console with consumer warmth.

### Layer 5: bottom proof strip inside the first viewport
At the lower edge of the hero, include one slim credibility / service strip. It can contain three items such as:
- Personal
- Business
- Nationwide / PNG connectivity story

Do not invent coverage percentages, uptime or customer counts.

If approved factual metrics are unavailable, use qualitative labels instead of fake statistics.

## Why this first viewport should work
A reviewer should understand the page in about five seconds:
- Telikom is modern.
- Telikom serves real customer needs.
- The website has useful actions.
- There is a strong campaign image.
- The page is not just another generic corporate mockup.

The action console creates the key structural difference from the old concepts.

## Page architecture
1. Integrated hero + Pulse Actions.
2. Product discovery strip: Mobile / Home Internet / Business / Remote.
3. Current Offers with no more than three featured items.
4. Service status / important notice module.
5. Why Telikom / PNG connectivity story.
6. Business and Government solutions.
7. Digital self-service / app / Self Care feature.
8. Help hub: support, FAQs, store / contact.
9. Latest News / Public Notices / Careers gateway.
10. Footer with formal corporate / regulatory links.

## Product discovery section
Immediately after the hero, shift from campaign to products without a visual reset that feels like another website.

Use four wide horizontal service lanes or a single segmented rail:
- Mobile
- Internet
- Business
- Remote Connectivity

On hover or selection, reveal 2 to 4 key sub-links. On mobile, these become stacked expandable rows.

Avoid a generic 4-card icon grid if possible.

## Offers section
Use at most three offer cards.

Each offer should include:
- clear product / plan name;
- one price or approved key fact;
- two or three benefits;
- one CTA.

The cards may use strong photography or typographic colour blocks, but should not all have identical blue backgrounds.

If live product data is not available, use obvious placeholder labels during prototyping and never imply they are real current offers.

## Government / SOE layer
This concept is consumer-first, but it still needs official credibility.

Include a compact institutional section later in the page:
- Important Notices
- News & Media
- Careers
- Tenders / Procurement if required
- Corporate Information

Do not make this look like a separate government portal. Use the same design system and strong information hierarchy.

## Service notice treatment
Government-owned utility websites often need urgent information. Build this into the system cleanly.

Normal state:
- a subtle horizontal "Service Updates" row below offers or near support.

Urgent state:
- a concise alert strip above the primary header.
- severity icon and plain language.
- link to details.

Do not show an outage warning in the mockup unless clearly labelled as a demonstration state.

## Visual system
### Palette
- Telikom Blue `#0875C9`
- Deep Ink `#102A3A`
- Sky Tint `#EAF6FD`
- Warm White `#FAFCFD`
- White `#FFFFFF`
- Neutral Rule `#D9E4EA`
- Optional electric accent: a lighter cyan derived from Telikom blue, used sparingly for interactive states

No green interface accent.

### Typography
Gibson preferred if licensed / already approved.

Use:
- bold, compact H1;
- clear 18 to 20px navigation / action typography where appropriate;
- short labels;
- large numerals only for approved pricing.

Avoid ultra-thin fashion typography.

### Geometry
- Action console: medium radius, not excessive.
- Campaign image: one major shape, not many floating tiles.
- Offers: moderate radius.
- Use straight alignment and large spacing to retain enterprise polish.

The design must not become a generic rounded-card SaaS landing page.

## Gen Z behaviour
Gen Z friendliness here comes from speed and confidence:
- immediate action;
- short copy;
- bold image crop;
- clear interactive feedback;
- mobile-first task completion;
- subtle kinetic transitions;
- no corporate wall of text.

Do not use slang, emojis, sticker graphics or youth stereotypes.

## Motion
Recommended:
- hero image has a subtle 600 to 900ms entry reveal;
- action rows respond with icon shift / highlight on hover;
- service rail selection animates underline or background movement;
- offer image can gently scale on hover;
- header compacts on scroll.

No auto-rotating hero.
No background particle system.
No excessive scroll-jacking.

Respect `prefers-reduced-motion`.

## PNG identity
Use real or approved PNG imagery wherever possible.

Potential themes:
- young PNG customer using mobile connectivity in an everyday setting;
- family / household connectivity;
- modern Port Moresby or Lae context;
- small business or creator;
- network infrastructure as secondary imagery.

The imagery should look contemporary and aspirational without feeling foreign.

## Business / enterprise section
Use a darker but not black section to change rhythm.

Headline direction:
**Connectivity built for organisations that keep PNG moving.**

Potential service groups:
- Business Internet
- Managed Connectivity
- Cloud / ICT if actually offered
- Remote / Satellite
- Government Solutions

Every item must use approved Telikom service terminology during production.

Include one clear "Talk to Business" CTA.

## Public / corporate content
Use an efficient newsroom-like grid near the lower page:
- latest Telikom news;
- important notice;
- careers / tenders / corporate gateway.

This is where government-site expectations can be satisfied without overwhelming consumers.

## Chatbot / help
Fixed bottom-right.

The launcher should be branded but restrained.

On open:
- Top Up help
- Internet help
- Mobile help
- Business enquiries
- Contact Telikom

Do not let the chatbot cover the Pulse Actions console on smaller screens.

## Mobile first viewport
This is a critical success criterion.

At approximately 390 x 844:
1. Header with logo and compact menu.
2. H1 and short copy.
3. Primary CTA.
4. Hero image cropped to a purposeful 16:10 or 4:3 treatment.
5. Horizontal or 2 x 2 quick-action block with at least three actions visible before the first full scroll.

If the action console is pushed far below the image on mobile, the concept fails.

A good solution is to partially overlap a white action sheet with the lower edge of the hero image, while keeping all text readable.

## Accessibility
- WCAG AA contrast minimum.
- 44px minimum touch targets.
- Semantic button / link elements.
- One H1.
- Visible keyboard focus.
- No action identified by colour only.
- Alt text for meaningful imagery.
- No critical text embedded inside the campaign image.
- Action console has logical reading order.

## SEO
Content-first semantics despite the visual polish:
- one descriptive H1;
- crawlable Mobile / Internet / Business links;
- structured navigation;
- meaningful page title and meta description;
- Open Graph / social metadata;
- schema where appropriate;
- descriptive image names / alt text;
- no JS-only navigation for critical actions.

## Performance
Target strong production feasibility.

- Hero image in AVIF / WebP with responsive sources.
- Preload only the actual LCP hero asset.
- CSS transitions before animation libraries.
- Lazy-load below-fold imagery.
- SVG icons.
- Avoid large video in the default hero.

## CMS model
Hero:
- eyebrow
- headline
- body
- CTA
- image

Quick actions:
- label
- icon
- URL
- priority

Offers:
- product type
- title
- approved price
- benefit list
- CTA
- media

Notices:
- status
- title
- date
- severity
- details URL

News / corporate links:
- category
- title
- date
- image
- URL

## Must avoid
- Copying Ooredoo visually.
- Making the action console look like online banking.
- A generic hero + four cards below it.
- Six equal primary actions.
- Deep blue over every surface.
- Tiny Telikom logo.
- Fake service statistics.
- Generic American / European stock photography.
- Auto carousel.
- Too much copy before products.

## Definition of success
A reviewer should be able to stay entirely within the first screen and still feel that the site is modern, useful and complete enough to deserve exploration.

The concept succeeds when the banner itself is no longer the only thing being judged. The first viewport becomes a coherent combination of campaign, utility and Telikom credibility.
