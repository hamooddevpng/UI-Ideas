# Design 33 — Infrastructure First

## Portfolio position
- Gen-Z: **2/10**
- Enterprise / premium: **10/10**
- UX clarity: **8/10**
- PNG identity: **8/10**
- Motion: **4/10**
- Complexity: **6/10**
- Similarity target: **0/10**

## Core premise
Lead with Telikom’s role as telecommunications infrastructure rather than consumer promotions. The design language should feel architectural, engineered and national — appropriate for enterprise, government and institutional audiences while still retaining clear consumer access.

This is not the same as Design 2. Design 2 still used a familiar telecom hero/services/plans structure. Design 33 should be built like an infrastructure/engineering publication.

## First viewport
No promotional hero.

Use:
- large Telikom logo and slogan;
- an architectural full-width/fixed-grid image of real telecommunications infrastructure or PNG landscape with infrastructure context;
- a concise statement about connectivity infrastructure;
- a diagrammatic service backbone showing Mobile / Fixed / Business / Remote;
- small but clear Consumer Services and Self Care entry points.

Avoid dark overlays; use image and adjacent typography.

## Page architecture
1. Infrastructure statement.
2. Network layers / service backbone.
3. Business & enterprise capabilities.
4. Remote/satellite connectivity.
5. Consumer gateway: Mobile + Home.
6. People / PNG outcomes story.
7. Technology / service operations.
8. Self Care/support.
9. News/corporate/footer.

The hierarchy intentionally prioritises infrastructure before retail products.

## Visual system
### Palette
- Telikom Blue `#0875C9`
- Structural Blue `#124869`
- Steel `#CEDFE8`
- Background `#F5F8FA`
- Ink `#122734`
- White `#FFFFFF`
- Rule `#C3D3DB`

### Typography
Gibson preferred. Large architectural headings, narrow labels, strong numerals if approved facts are used.

### Graphics
Technical line diagrams, section cuts, maps/topology and photography. Avoid sci-fi glows. Visualisations should feel engineering-led.

## Data integrity
Do not invent infrastructure counts, fibre lengths, uptime, bandwidth or coverage. Use only approved facts. Where a diagram is conceptual, label it as a service architecture rather than a literal network map.

## Service backbone
Present Mobile, Fixed/Home, Enterprise and Remote as interconnected network layers. Clicking a layer reveals service families and links. Consumer navigation remains directly accessible from header.

## Business
This is a flagship business concept. Use structured service taxonomy and strong contact CTA. Explain benefits in plain language; avoid jargon overload.

## Consumer
Because the homepage is infrastructure-led, create an explicit “For individuals and households” gateway around the middle of the page with Mobile Data, Home Internet, Devices and Self Care. Do not force ordinary users through enterprise content.

## PNG identity
Use infrastructure within Papua New Guinea as the visual context: terrain, urban centres, remote areas, professionals. Avoid generic data-centre stock unless it corresponds to a real Telikom service/context.

## Motion
Subtle diagram line drawing, image reveal, layer highlighting. No 3D or cinematic effects.

## Chatbot
Professional fixed bottom-right with separate shortcuts for Personal Support and Business Enquiries.

## Mobile
Reorder content so quick consumer/support actions remain near top, even if desktop narrative prioritises infrastructure. Backbone diagram becomes vertical layered list.

## Accessibility
- Conceptual diagrams have text equivalents.
- No essential copy embedded in image.
- High contrast.
- One H1.
- Clear landmarks and focus states.
- Large targets.

## SEO
Use real headings/service terms, not only institutional language. Ensure Mobile, Home Internet, Business Solutions and Remote/VSAT paths are crawlable and easy to find.

## Performance
Moderate. Use SVG for diagrams, responsive images, minimal JS. No need for WebGL.

## CMS
CMS manages service layers, approved infrastructure facts, stories and news. Diagram structure should be controlled by code/design system to prevent misleading ad-hoc network visuals.

## Must avoid
- Design 2’s hero + service cards + plan table repetition.
- Fake metrics.
- Military/command-centre aesthetics.
- All-dark navy design.
- Neglecting everyday customer tasks.

## Success
Enterprise/government viewers should immediately understand Telikom as serious infrastructure, while consumers can still find practical services without friction.
