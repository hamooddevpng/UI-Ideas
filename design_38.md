# Design 38 - Signal One

## Status
New-round concept specification for `38.html`. This is the premium campaign extreme of designs 35 to 38.

## Portfolio position
- Gen Z affinity: **9/10**
- Enterprise / premium: **10/10**
- UX clarity: **8/10**
- PNG identity: **8/10**
- Above-the-fold utility: **9/10**
- Motion: **8/10**
- Code complexity: **8/10**
- Similarity target vs designs 1 to 34: **0/10 in campaign art direction and utility structure**

## Core premise
Create one unforgettable Telikom hero rather than another conventional telecom homepage banner.

The page should feel like a top-tier brand campaign from a major global operator, with the confidence and restraint of an enterprise company. The hero uses one central visual idea, one headline and one CTA. Practical customer actions are not mixed into the campaign copy. They live in a precise utility dock that remains visible inside the same first viewport.

This separation is important:
- the campaign earns attention;
- the utility dock earns usefulness;
- the rest of the page earns trust.

## Creative direction
Use the concept of a single continuous "signal" as the art-direction device.

This is not a literal radio-wave illustration.

A single Telikom-blue graphic path can travel through the hero composition and interact with photography / type. It can frame a person, become a crop boundary, underline the headline, connect to the utility dock and continue subtly into the next section.

The signal should be clean, geometric and brand-owned.

Do not build a glowing sci-fi waveform.

## First viewport
### Header
Extremely clean and premium.

Left:
- large Telikom logo and readable slogan.

Middle:
- Mobile
- Internet
- Business
- About

Right:
- Search
- Support
- Self Care

A small Personal / Business toggle may sit above the main row if needed.

The header should almost disappear visually behind the strength of the campaign, while remaining fully usable.

### Hero composition
Use one full-width, art-directed stage with a light or warm-neutral background rather than defaulting to blue.

The visual can combine:
- one PNG person / group with strong photographic direction;
- one bold Telikom-blue signal path;
- oversized headline;
- generous whitespace.

Example copy structure only:

**H1:** One signal. More possible.

Support copy should stay under roughly 14 to 18 words.

Primary CTA:
Discover Telikom

Secondary link:
Explore Plans

The H1 should be large enough to feel campaign-grade, but not so large that mobile becomes unusable.

### Utility dock
At the bottom of the first viewport, place a full-width dock floating slightly over the campaign edge or structurally attached to it.

Label:
**Quick actions**

Four actions:
- Top Up / Recharge
- Self Care
- Internet
- Support

A fifth text link can point to Business.

The dock should have strong contrast and large targets. It can be white over a pale hero or deep ink over white, depending on the selected image.

Avoid a glassmorphism effect. The dock should feel solid, premium and intentional.

### Optional live information slot
Reserve one small area in the dock for:
- Important Update;
- Service Notice;
- Latest offer.

Only one item at a time.

Do not fabricate live service status in the prototype.

## Banner acceptance criteria
Before any below-fold work is considered successful, the hero must pass all of these:

- Telikom brand is unmistakable.
- Logo and slogan are readable.
- One message dominates.
- Photography feels contemporary and PNG-relevant.
- The layout does not resemble a generic stock telecom template.
- Four useful customer actions are visible.
- Enterprise polish is obvious.
- The viewer can understand the composition in under five seconds.
- There is no need to wait for a carousel slide.

## Why this is Gen Z friendly
The direction uses contemporary brand-language principles:
- one strong idea;
- confident cropping;
- reduced copy;
- bold typography;
- expressive but controlled graphic motion;
- quick task access;
- clean mobile composition.

It avoids the false assumption that youth design needs maximal colour, stickers or novelty UI.

## Why this is enterprise friendly
The design system stays disciplined:
- restrained palette;
- consistent grid;
- professional imagery;
- clear CTA hierarchy;
- strong accessibility;
- no playful slang;
- room for corporate and government content below the campaign.

It should feel credible in a board presentation as well as on a young customer's phone.

## Page architecture
1. Signal One campaign hero + utility dock.
2. Service index integrated with the continuing signal line.
3. Featured Mobile / Internet offers.
4. "Connecting PNG" national story.
5. Business & Government solutions.
6. Self Care / digital service feature.
7. Service Updates and Important Notices.
8. Innovation / Technology / Network capability story.
9. News / Media / Careers / Tenders gateway where required.
10. Footer.

## Section 2: service index
Carry the signal graphic out of the hero and use it as a subtle structural guide.

A large typographic list:

01 Mobile
02 Internet
03 Business
04 Remote Connectivity
05 Support

Selecting / hovering a service reveals a single supporting image and short description.

This should feel editorial, not like five separate cards.

On mobile, use stacked rows with optional image expansion.

## Offers section
Now allow stronger commercial content.

Use one large featured offer and two smaller offers rather than three equal cards.

Layout example:
- 60 percent featured offer;
- 40 percent column containing two smaller offers.

The asymmetry supports the campaign identity and lets management prioritise one offer.

Every offer needs:
- approved plan / product title;
- price or key benefit;
- concise supporting detail;
- CTA.

No fake data.

## Connecting PNG story
Use a white or pale background after the commercial area to restore breathing space.

Headline direction:
**A network has meaning when it connects people to what matters.**

Show three contexts:
- People / households
- Business / government
- Remote / national connectivity

Use real approved Telikom stories or neutral placeholder copy during prototype stage.

Avoid unverified coverage claims.

## Business & Government
Use a highly polished enterprise section with a grid that could sit comfortably on Telefónica / Orange-quality corporate websites.

Possible hierarchy:
- strong headline;
- one paragraph maximum;
- 3 to 5 service links;
- contact CTA;
- one infrastructure / professional image.

Headline direction:
**Serious connectivity for serious work.**

This section must not use playful consumer campaign treatments.

## Government / SOE information layer
Borrow the clarity of PNG government service sites, not their visual styling.

Create a structured official-information band:

### Important Notices
Most recent high-priority notice.

### News & Media
Latest approved news.

### Corporate
About, governance / leadership if required, publications.

### Opportunities
Careers, tenders / procurement if required.

Use dates and clear category labels.

This content should look trustworthy and easy to scan.

## Utility dock behaviour after scroll
On desktop, the full dock does not need to stay sticky forever.

When the hero scrolls away, it can collapse into a smaller sticky action button / short utility strip containing:
- Self Care;
- Top Up;
- Support.

Do not consume too much vertical space.

On mobile, consider a bottom action bar only if it does not conflict with browser controls or chatbot placement.

## Visual system
### Palette
- Telikom Blue `#0875C9`
- Signal Blue Light `#5AB7EA`
- Deep Ink `#102A38`
- Paper `#F8FAFB`
- Warm White `#FDFDFC`
- White `#FFFFFF`
- Fine Rule `#D7E1E5`

Telikom blue should be highly recognisable but concentrated in the signal graphic, CTAs and interaction states.

No green accent.

### Typography
Gibson preferred.

Hero typography:
- bold;
- short;
- large;
- tightly art-directed.

Body copy:
- calm;
- readable;
- generous line height.

Service index numbers can use a narrow or regular weight of the same family rather than adding an unnecessary second typeface.

### Geometry
The signal path provides most of the visual shape.

Other components should remain simple:
- low / medium radius;
- strong rectangles;
- wide image crops;
- disciplined alignment.

Do not combine the signal line with a page full of blobs, circles and floating cards.

## Signal graphic implementation
Preferred: SVG.

Rules:
- scalable;
- responsive;
- no raster blur;
- can animate stroke / mask lightly;
- must not obstruct content;
- on small screens, simplify rather than forcing the desktop path into a narrow viewport.

The SVG must be decorative unless it conveys meaningful content.

## Motion
This concept has one signature animation: the signal path subtly draws / reveals during initial load or when entering the hero.

Duration should be controlled, approximately 800 to 1400ms, without delaying content availability.

Other motion:
- headline / image reveal;
- utility dock rises 8 to 16px into place;
- service index image changes on hover / selection;
- CTA feedback.

No looping hero animation.
No autoplay carousel.
No continuous glowing pulse.
No scroll-jacking.

Reduced-motion mode shows the final composition immediately.

## Photography
Campaign photography must be exceptional.

Prefer:
- contemporary PNG people;
- expressive but natural pose;
- clean wardrobe / styling;
- real device interaction when appropriate;
- clear subject separation for art direction;
- enough negative space for the signal graphic and text.

Avoid:
- corporate handshake imagery;
- generic offshore call centre imagery;
- heavy blue colour grading;
- visibly AI-like anatomy / devices in production assets;
- cliché tower-on-mountain hero unless it is genuinely compelling and approved.

## Mobile first viewport
At 390 x 844:
- compact header;
- campaign label;
- large but controlled H1;
- main image / subject;
- primary CTA;
- utility dock with four actions arranged 2 x 2 or horizontal scroll;
- the blue signal path simplified into one clean curve / edge.

Do not let the artistic treatment push utility below two full screen heights.

The mobile hero must be independently art-directed with its own image crop and signal path.

## Accessibility
- H1 remains real HTML text.
- Signal SVG is `aria-hidden` when decorative.
- Utility actions have visible labels.
- Contrast meets WCAG AA.
- Focus states remain obvious on light and dark surfaces.
- 44px targets.
- No text depends on motion to appear.
- Image subject does not sit behind critical text at responsive breakpoints.
- Important notices use semantic status / alert patterns only when appropriate.

## SEO
The campaign copy can be brief because service discovery immediately follows in stable HTML.

Required:
- descriptive title / meta description;
- crawlable service index;
- one H1;
- service H2s below;
- Organization / Breadcrumb / product schema only where valid;
- local PNG context in approved descriptive copy;
- news / notice pages crawlable;
- alt text for meaningful campaign photography.

## Performance
The signature visual must not become a performance problem.

- SVG signal graphic kept lean.
- One optimized hero image.
- No hero video by default.
- No animation framework unless implementation complexity truly requires it.
- Lazy-load all below-fold images.
- Compress fonts and reuse existing Telikom font assets.
- Keep LCP image dimensions explicit.

## CMS model
Campaign hero:
- campaign label
- headline
- body
- primary CTA
- secondary link
- desktop image
- mobile image
- alt text

Utility dock:
- up to four primary actions
- one business link
- optional notice slot

Featured offers:
- rank / priority
- title
- benefits
- price / approved value
- CTA
- media

Official content:
- type
- headline
- date
- priority
- destination

## Must avoid
- Turning the signal line into a cliché sine wave.
- Dark cyberpunk design.
- Generic telecom gradient background.
- Hero carousel.
- Glassmorphism utility dock.
- Too many decorative shapes.
- Hiding actions for the sake of art direction.
- Tiny campaign copy that looks like a fashion portfolio rather than a service website.
- Fake network / coverage claims.
- Copying Orange, Telefónica or STC composition directly.

## Definition of success
This should be the design management remembers after reviewing four options.

It succeeds if the first viewport feels like a real Telikom campaign that could run across web, social and outdoor media, while still allowing a customer to Top Up, reach Self Care, find Internet or get Support immediately.

The page should prove that "fresh" and "enterprise" are not opposites.
