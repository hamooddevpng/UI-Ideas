# Design 36 - The PNG Connection

## Status
New-round concept specification for `36.html`. This direction starts from the requirement for a fresh, enterprise-grade Telikom identity with a stronger Papua New Guinea presence.

## Portfolio position
- Gen Z affinity: **8/10**
- Enterprise / premium: **9/10**
- UX clarity: **9/10**
- PNG identity: **10/10**
- Above-the-fold utility: **9/10**
- Motion: **6/10**
- Code complexity: **7/10**
- Similarity target vs designs 1 to 34: **0/10 in hero composition**

## Core premise
Make the first viewport feel unmistakably Papua New Guinean without turning Telikom into a tourism website or decorating the interface with unapproved cultural motifs.

The visual idea is a powerful contemporary PNG photograph paired with a permanent vertical action rail. The photography carries emotion and national identity. The action rail carries utility. Strong typography and restrained geometry carry enterprise credibility.

This is a national-brand homepage with consumer speed.

## Design thesis
Global telecom sites can look polished but geographically interchangeable. Telikom has an advantage they do not: it can visually own its place in Papua New Guinea.

The homepage should communicate:
- local relevance;
- modern connectivity;
- national purpose;
- everyday usefulness;
- serious enterprise capability.

The concept should feel suitable for a young customer buying data and for an institution evaluating Telikom as a strategic connectivity provider.

## First viewport
Use a full-width composition divided into three functional zones.

### Zone A: header
A clean light header with:
- Telikom logo and slogan large enough to read;
- Mobile;
- Internet;
- Business;
- Support;
- About;
- Search;
- Self Care.

A small Personal / Business audience switch can sit above or within the header.

Avoid a giant mega-menu visible by default.

### Zone B: national image stage
The main 70 to 78 percent of the viewport is a single striking PNG image or image composition.

Possible visual directions:
- young PNG people in an urban / connected setting;
- a family in a modern household using connectivity naturally;
- a small business team with recognisable PNG context;
- infrastructure and landscape integrated into one campaign photograph;
- a carefully directed image that moves from human foreground to landscape / network context in the distance.

Do not use a dark overlay. Copy should occupy a clean area of the photograph or sit on an adjacent light block.

Example copy structure only:

**Eyebrow:** Telikom Papua New Guinea

**H1:** PNG moves when we stay connected.

Short support line.

Primary CTA: Explore Telikom
Secondary CTA: View Services

### Zone C: persistent action rail
A narrow 22 to 30 percent column on the right or left, visually attached to the image but on a solid light surface.

Label:
**Quick access**

Actions:
- Top Up / Recharge
- Self Care
- Mobile & Data
- Internet
- Support

At the bottom of the rail:
**For Business & Government →**

This is not a floating card. It should feel like a deliberate piece of the page architecture.

## First-viewport detail: national proof line
At the bottom of the image stage, include a quiet line such as:
**Connecting people, homes, businesses and communities across Papua New Guinea.**

Do not attach a number unless it is approved and verifiable.

This gives the hero enterprise / public-purpose weight without turning it into a corporate report.

## What makes this different from Design 31 PNG Modernism
Design 31 used abstract geometry and local visual rhythm as the identity system.

Design 36 must instead use:
- photographic art direction;
- real people / places;
- editorial image cropping;
- one strong action rail;
- almost no decorative patterning.

The page should feel like a premium national campaign, not a graphic-design study.

## What makes this different from Design 33 Infrastructure First
Design 33 prioritised infrastructure and engineering.

Design 36 prioritises human connection and place. Infrastructure may support the story, but people and national relevance lead.

## Page architecture
1. National campaign hero + Quick Access rail.
2. "Choose how you connect" service selector.
3. Personal connectivity: Mobile + Internet.
4. Business and Government capability.
5. PNG story / national role feature.
6. Offers / latest products.
7. Service Updates / Important Notices.
8. Digital help and Self Care.
9. News / community / corporate content.
10. Careers / tenders / publications gateway where required.
11. Footer.

## Section 2: Choose how you connect
Use three very large editorial choices rather than a standard card grid:

### Mobile
For calls, data and everyday connectivity.

### Home / Internet
For households and fixed connectivity.

### Business & Government
For organisations and larger connectivity needs.

Each choice occupies a wide image-and-type panel. On desktop, three panels can sit in a horizontal strip. On mobile, stack them with the title always visible.

Do not use small generic line icons as the main visual.

## PNG story module
This is a defining section.

Headline direction:
**Built for the way Papua New Guinea connects.**

Use a story sequence with two or three images:
- city / enterprise;
- home / community;
- remote / infrastructure.

Copy should be concise and factual. Avoid broad claims that cannot be substantiated.

A horizontal visual timeline can show service contexts, not fake historical milestones.

## Government / SOE consideration
PNG state-owned and government websites commonly need official content that commercial telecom sites may not emphasise.

Create a clean "Telikom Information" band later in the homepage with links such as:
- Important Notices
- News & Media
- Careers
- Tenders / Procurement
- Corporate Information
- Contact

Only include categories that Telikom actually requires.

The visual treatment should be authoritative but still part of the same modern design.

## Public-service interaction pattern
For high-priority notices:
- show a slim status strip below the header;
- use plain-language title;
- include date / status;
- make details one click away.

For normal notices:
- place them below products in a compact list.

Avoid modal popups for routine notices.

## Enterprise section
This section should feel more serious than the consumer modules without becoming an entirely different website.

Use a dark ink background with strong white type and one bright image.

Headline direction:
**Connectivity for the organisations shaping PNG.**

Service links can include only approved capabilities, for example:
- Business Connectivity
- Enterprise Internet
- Remote Connectivity
- Government Solutions
- Managed / ICT services if applicable

CTA:
**Talk to Telikom Business**

## Offers
Keep promotional cards visually secondary to the national hero.

Use 2 to 3 offer stories with large price / benefit typography and human-scale imagery.

The offer area should feel modern and youthful, perhaps with asymmetrical widths, but it must not become a noisy ecommerce grid.

## Visual system
### Palette
- Telikom Blue `#0875C9`
- Deep Ink `#0E2938`
- Cloud `#F4F8FA`
- Warm White `#FCFDFD`
- White `#FFFFFF`
- Soft Blue `#DDEFF9`
- Neutral Border `#D6E0E5`

No green interface accent.

### Typography
Gibson preferred.

Use:
- large confident headlines;
- medium-weight body text;
- strong geographic / section labels;
- large whitespace.

The typographic feeling should be closer to a contemporary national brand campaign than a government portal.

### Photography
Photography quality is the primary determinant of this concept's success.

Requirements:
- PNG relevance must be believable;
- people should feel current, not staged in outdated corporate poses;
- images should be high-resolution and naturally coloured;
- skin tones must be rendered accurately;
- avoid excessive blue tinting;
- avoid generic foreign data-centre imagery in the hero;
- avoid tokenistic cultural styling.

If authentic approved photography is unavailable, use clearly provisional photography during mockup review and replace it before production.

## Graphic language
Keep graphics minimal.

Allowed:
- thin signal lines;
- map-like coordinate labels without claiming literal network coverage;
- small location labels;
- simple directional rules;
- restrained Telikom-blue shapes used as framing.

Avoid:
- fake glowing network maps;
- node constellations over the entire country;
- traditional motifs used decoratively without context;
- 3D telecom towers floating in space.

## Gen Z friendliness
This concept should appeal through art direction rather than visual gimmicks.

Use:
- strong full-bleed image crop;
- oversized but readable headline;
- concise language;
- animated action rail states;
- smooth image / text transitions;
- modern mobile navigation;
- editorial section pacing.

No slang. No stickers. No forced youth imagery.

## Motion
- Image enters with subtle masked reveal.
- Action rail hover changes the associated micro-label or icon.
- Service selector panels can expand slightly on hover.
- Section image transitions should be soft and controlled.
- Header can become compact on scroll.

No parallax that harms readability.
No autoplay video as the default.
Respect reduced motion.

## Mobile first viewport
At 390 x 844, do not preserve the desktop side rail literally.

Recommended sequence:
1. compact header;
2. headline + CTA over / adjacent to hero image;
3. image occupying around 40 to 45 percent of viewport height;
4. Quick Access becomes a horizontally scrollable action dock or 2 x 2 action block partially overlapping the image bottom;
5. Business / Government gateway stays visible near the first scroll boundary.

At least three useful actions should be visible without a long scroll.

## Accessibility
- Never rely on text over visually busy image areas without a solid / controlled background.
- Strong contrast.
- Keyboard-focus states.
- 44px targets.
- Semantic hierarchy.
- One H1.
- Descriptive labels and image alternatives.
- Action rail order follows DOM reading order.
- No motion required to understand content.

## SEO
The emotional hero must still lead into crawlable service content.

Required:
- Mobile / Internet / Business links in HTML;
- service-focused H2s;
- descriptive copy about Telikom in PNG;
- approved organization schema;
- proper metadata;
- crawlable News / Notices / Careers gateways;
- meaningful alt text for PNG imagery.

## Performance
This design is image-heavy, so image discipline is mandatory.

- One LCP hero image only.
- Art-directed `picture` sources for desktop and mobile.
- AVIF / WebP.
- Lazy-load all later story imagery.
- Avoid video until after performance testing.
- Use CSS / lightweight JS for rail interactions.

## Must avoid
- Tourism-site aesthetics.
- Unapproved cultural motifs.
- Generic global-city stock imagery.
- Fake network maps.
- Heavy blue photo overlays.
- Hiding useful actions below a cinematic image.
- Making the page so institutional that young consumers feel it is not for them.
- Making the page so trendy that enterprise / government audiences lose trust.

## Definition of success
The first reaction should be: "This is Telikom in Papua New Guinea, and it looks world-class."

The second reaction should be: "I can immediately find what I need."

If both happen before the first scroll, this concept has done its job.
