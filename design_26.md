# Design 26 — Service Exchange

## Portfolio position
- Gen-Z: **6/10**
- Enterprise / premium: **7/10**
- UX clarity: **10/10**
- PNG identity: **5/10**
- Motion: **3/10**
- Complexity: **5/10**
- Similarity target: **1/10**

## Core premise
Remove the hero entirely. The homepage starts immediately as a clear service marketplace/directory. The visual statement is radical simplicity: Telikom respects the visitor’s time.

This is the opposite of the cinematic concepts. Distinctiveness comes from having **no promotional first screen**.

## First viewport
Header with large logo/slogan and utilities. Immediately below: a service index organized by customer need, with enough content visible above the fold to choose a path.

Possible top-level columns/rows:
- Mobile
- Home Internet
- Business
- Remote Connectivity
- Devices
- Self Care & Support

A slim current-promotion/news strip may exist, but it cannot dominate.

## Page architecture
1. Service exchange / master directory.
2. Quick tasks.
3. Compare popular plans/services.
4. “For your home / for your business / for remote sites” pathways.
5. PNG people/network feature.
6. News and announcements.
7. Support/locations.
8. Footer.

## Directory interaction
Provide filters or tabs for Personal / Home / Business / Support, but keep all entries crawlable. Each service row includes title, short description and explicit action. Avoid icons unless they materially improve scanning.

## Visual system
### Palette
- Telikom Blue `#0875C9`
- Deep Blue `#0766A6`
- Soft Blue `#E2F1F9`
- Background `#F9FBFC`
- Ink `#193A4C`
- White `#FFFFFF`
- Line `#D1E0E8`

### Style
Quiet, highly legible, structured. Use rules, columns and whitespace more than cards. Think public-service clarity plus modern ecommerce navigation.

## Typography
Gibson preferred. Service names 20–28px; descriptions 14–16px. Strong hierarchy, minimal decorative type.

## Plans
Use a true comparison table/row system rather than marketing cards. Mobile must transform tables responsibly into labelled rows, not force tiny columns.

## PNG identity
Because the interface is utility-heavy, reserve one large editorial PNG story section mid-page. It should break the directory rhythm and remind visitors of Telikom’s national/local role.

## Motion
Extremely restrained: row hover, filter transitions, accordion expansion. This design should feel fast and calm.

## Chatbot
Standard fixed bottom-right support panel. Keep greeting concise. The service directory already answers discovery questions; chatbot is for assistance.

## Mobile
This should be one of the best-performing mobile concepts:
- service categories as large accordion/list sections;
- sticky quick-task bar;
- filter chips only where useful;
- no large hero to scroll past.

## Accessibility
Strong by nature. Use real lists/navigation landmarks, explicit links, accessible accordions, visible focus. One H1 can introduce “Telikom services” in a compact top section.

## SEO
Excellent. All key services are immediately present as descriptive crawlable links. Maintain one keyword-focused H1, useful H2 category headings, internal links and approved structured data.

## Performance
Very light. CSS-first, minimal JS. Responsive images only in editorial/news sections. This concept should serve as a benchmark for performance and maintainability.

## CMS
Excellent CMS fit. Service Directory is data-driven; editors update titles/descriptions/URLs. Promotions/news optional. Directory categories should be managed but constrained to preserve usability.

## Must avoid
- Adding a giant banner later because the page feels “too empty.”
- Filling every row with decorative icons/cards.
- Dense enterprise jargon.
- Hiding services behind mega-menu only.
- Copying the current site’s visual style just because both are directory-oriented.

## Success
Visitors can reach a service faster than in any other concept. The absence of a hero should itself be a clear creative statement.
